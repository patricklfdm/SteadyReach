# Reviewing a 1D river reach

**English** | [简体中文](RIVER_REVIEW_CN.md) | [River workflow](RIVER_REACH_EN.md)

## On the geometry preview

Open **Section directions**. Each cut is compared with the normal to the mapped channel using two distances tied to adjacent section spacing. A turn that follows a bend can remain in place. Different local and wider directions identify a bend worth inspecting. Consistent skew (at least 22° locally and 18° at the wider scale, with directions agreeing within 12°) qualifies for a bounded rotation search. Those thresholds guide inspection. Rotation is limited to 30° and requires a unique centerline crossing, finite DEM elevations and compatibility with nearby sections. For a computed reach, candidate rotations are also screened against the previous native stage at previously dry sections. Recheck all endpoints after the new solve. A locked bank selection can still move its station label when a straight cut is extended to the left. Its map position is preserved.

Use each **section profile** to compare the proposed left and right banks, their competing stations and slope-break support across three scales. A distinct, consistent pair may be staged in the editable draft. Terraces, competing shoulders, weak agreement and structures can require judgment. A sampled DEM bank is an inference. Click a different point or type its station to choose another location.

A dry section end may stop short of a second floodplain flow path. After a Q100 solve, SteadyReach checks large changes in native left/right flow share between neighbors. Where evidence warrants, it samples a strip between their same-side overbanks and tests for a connected low route at the lower of the two stages and with an interpolated stage. Missing elevations, an intervening ridge or a stage-dependent path are reported separately. Inspect imagery, levees, secondary channels and the neighboring profiles. The **lateral coverage** alternative requires an explicit choice before it can be adopted. Only a new hydraulic calculation can show the effect of the proposed footprint.

## Decide what to check first after a solve

Open **Results → Delivery assessment → First action**. SteadyReach checks the native result and both packages first. If a tested boundary moves water levels in your selected reach, the action opens the search for independent control. For a geometry alternative it opens the affected section and geometry. Each recommendation identifies whether it concerns the adopted model or a candidate.

- **Boundary evidence.** Open the field search to see the gage identifier, downstream distance, rating range in cfs, applicable dates, datum conflicts and out-of-period peaks. A tabulated stage bracket in feet applies at that gage. Transferring it to the model requires a physical basis and uncertainty. A matched, dated downstream observation with a positive reported uncertainty provides an interval for that event. Preserve the source and date when setting up a sensitivity run.
- **Independent observations.** Expand the residual finding to see each affected station, event date, signed difference and missing or exceeded uncertainty. A small residual alone cannot quantify survey accuracy. Obtain point-level records, vertical control and the identity of measured versus interpolated points where needed.
- **Floodplain flow.** Under **Inspect floodplain and boundary response by section**, choose the geometry, profile and RS. Compare each side with its two adjacent cuts. The map shades the tested strips. The table reports native flow shares, connectivity at the lower or interpolated water level, missing DEM cells and ground elevation sampled along mapped road or levee lines inside the strip. Folded or materially bent cuts require map review. A share change of at least 15 percentage points with one side carrying at least 20% flags an abrupt partition. Check openings, lateral exchange and the local suitability of 1D flow using surveys or field records. Terrain along a road gives surface elevations and cannot establish an opening invert or capacity.

## Add field feedback and deliver an independent scenario

In **Results → Add field feedback or export a request**, download the site request or the prefilled point table. The table uses one row per original observation and includes its event, date, station, role, elevation, datum, discharge and source. When the site has only a nearby streamgage, the request identifies the downstream RS and the required transfer. Import located event observations through **Field observations** before attempting an event run. Switching events restores the saved rows and attachment for that event.

1. Select the event. Apply one set of feedback to all its points, the boundary, or the other points. For different answers per point, edit the last four columns of the CSV and import it once. Classify point identity as measured, interpolated or unknown, then classify the feedback as documented, assumed or unknown. Use positive vertical uncertainty in **feet**. Attach the field record for documented values. Missing identity or uncertainty remains visible.
2. Save and preview. Review the station rows, evidence classes, applicable flow and the three downstream stages. **EventBase**, **TailLow** and **TailHigh** use the same event flow and geometry. The low and high stages use the boundary observation minus and plus its uncertainty. A nearby rating by itself supplies no boundary. If the active upstream or downstream controls are unsupported, resolve the listed issue before computing.
3. Confirm once to create a child project under the original saved project. The actual steady-flow plan contains all three profiles. The result page, bilingual reports, package opening instructions and Field_review.json identify assumed feedback, the signed peak change, threshold count and affected stations. The downstream control is excluded from independent residuals. An assumed scenario leaves field verification open.
4. For a completed geometry comparison, open **Inspect floodplain and boundary response by section**, select an RS, then **Compare overbank paths and record a decision**. Baseline and candidate maps use the same extent. Compare stage, left/right flow shares, checked strips and mapped crossings. Choose the geometry or keep the baseline pending survey or 2D review. Enter the condition to print, save the preview and confirm the independent edition. The source run remains available. Both packages identify the selection and conditions. A mapped crossing alone does not establish invert elevation or hydraulic capacity.

## Before and after a calculation

On the parameter page, inspect the **key river inputs** including Q, channel and overbank roughness, bed representation and end conditions. Check the stated basis and competing values. If the only bed evidence is a surface DEM, report the calculation as conditional. Use the final confirmation when the displayed inputs describe the intended case.

On the result page open **Suggested next steps**. Expand a group to see the affected stations and native codes. **Profile and neighbors** opens the computed station with its adjacent reaches visible for comparison. **Comparison with previous calculation** shows water levels, changes in bank positions and Q100 left/right flow shares at matching centers. A small change in stage may coexist with a large redistribution of overbank flow.

For a boundary or roughness sensitivity run, select a range tied to the site, review the proposed cases and confirm the batch. The results give the largest signed difference, its station, and the list of stations above the internal 0.10 ft screen for each tested profile. A zero response applies only to that tested range and model. Use observed flood levels or surveyed profiles for independent validation.

## How many operator actions?

Expand **Recorded operations for this project edition**. The count measures successful requests, such as adopting a layout, saving edited fields and confirming computation. A single batch consent can start multiple calculations. Older editions may have only partial history. Reading a map, discussing an assumption, reading external studies, elapsed review time and individual mouse clicks are outside the count. The saved receipts hold the route and operation counts and omit entered parameter values and reviewer identity.
